## How to read this (limits of the comparison)

- The spike does not decide; RG-178 adds sectors and applies the decision rule. FAIL rows would stay FAIL here.
- c1 / c2 compare unequal content: A builds a whole WorldGen world per region (terrain model, city streets, villages, motorway, profiles, carve data), B only anchors (settlements as points + corridor centrelines + segment profiles). No street content, no terrain carve, no sector meshes in B (out of scope, RG-178). B's cost is the cost of the global skeleton, not of a drivable world.
- n4 on B's side: the ground is the planet surface + a global detail layer of the WorldGen size (`Ground`, about +-11 m at 64-256 m cells), so grades are measured on comparable relief. Grade and radius limits hold by construction (grade cap 0.9 x class max, fillets >= class minimum); the informative B numbers are the vertical-curve counts and the share of straight corridors.
- Straightness: almost every B corridor is a single straight chord between two settlements (Notes). A real road of these classes bends every few hundred metres (fitted WorldGen straight runs, p50 51-85 m for secondary to motorway). The cost rows are not affected (station count depends on length only), the look is: RG-178 needs a meander / leg-length rule.
- n1 / n2 / n3 on A's side are what the region pipeline delivers today: every region is its own world, roads end at the region border (cut by the 32 m taper), and nothing joins across a border unless two endpoints coincide within 1 m (n2 shows how far that carries).
- n5 is a property of B only: A's determinism is covered by the RG-175 hash test (unchanged, passes).
